About

Computer Engineering, Stevens Institute of Technology. Edwin A. Stevens Scholar. NCAA Division III varsity baseball.

I'm a Computer Engineering student at Stevens Institute of Technology in Hoboken, New Jersey (entered Fall 2023), a recipient of the Edwin A. Stevens Scholarship, and an NCAA Division III varsity baseball player.

Baseball is where my engineering started. My first real program, written in my first semester, tried to compute the best approach for a hitter from the game situation and the pitcher's arsenal. The question it asked (how do you turn uncertain information into a decision?) has stayed with me through neural networks, Bayesian forecasting and stochastic simulation.

Lately the question has become physical. If computation is moving data and multiplying numbers, which substrate does that best? I've been working on it from two directions: digital hardware (FPGA design in SystemVerilog, aimed at specialised low-latency inference) and optical computing (training programmable photonic volumes with adjoint methods).

How I explore

Each of these started outside a classroom and turned into projects. Follow the links for the work itself.

Baseball

→ decisions under uncertainty

As a Division III player, I wanted to know what I should be trying to do at the plate given the situation and the pitcher. BOA turned that into a weighted scoring engine my teammates used. The same question, how to turn uncertain information into a decision, led to Bayesian networks and stochastic simulation.

  • BOA: Batter's Optimal Approach
  • Smart Traffic Intersection Simulation

Markets

→ honest forecasting

Markets are the hardest test I know for a model. QuantumPath combines Bayesian networks, generative models and path-integral weighting, and the memecoin work collected and simulated on-chain data. The honest result so far is no consistent edge, and that finding shaped where the work went next.

  • QuantumPath (Darke)
  • Solana Memecoin Trading Research

Physics

→ computing with light

Through NASA-funded research with the NJ Space Grant Consortium, I simulated programmable optical volumes trained with adjoint methods. It came from asking which physical substrate is best at the multiply-and-move-data work that machine learning needs.

  • Adjoint-Trained 3D Photonic Neural Network
  • Neural Network from Scratch

Hardware

→ low-latency inference

Digital hardware is the other side of the same question. I started with counters, FSMs and SPI drivers on an Artix-7 and am now building a low-latency AI accelerator with high-level synthesis. Robots and sensor stations taught me what happens when code meets the physical world.

  • Low-Latency HLS AI Accelerator
  • Autonomous Arena Robot
  • IoT Monitoring Station
