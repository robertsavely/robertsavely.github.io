The idea

In my first month at Stevens I was meeting dozens of people through teammates, classmates and friends of friends. I kept a spreadsheet of everyone and who introduced me. The idea behind Seven Connections Away was an app built on that one extra field. If every contact records who introduced them, your network stops being a list and becomes a graph. Then you can ask: how far away is anyone, and who's the chain of people who can introduce us?

It never became an app. The spreadsheet was the prototype. Here is the data model, anonymised:

The data model

Each row was a person with five fields that mattered: name, link (the person who introduced them), contact details, birthday and free-text notes ("baseball team, outfielder", "mech e", "from Singapore"). The link column turns the sheet into a directed tree rooted at me:

G=(V,E),(u→v)∈E  ⟺  u introduced me to vG = (V, E), \qquad (u \to v) \in E \iff u \text{ introduced me to } vG=(V,E),(u→v)∈E⟺u introduced me to v

The degree of separation of a person is their depth in that tree: the number of introductions between us, found by breadth-first search from the root. In this snapshot: 26 people at one introduction, 23 at two, 7 at three, 3 at four.

Why "seven"

The six-degrees idea is a branching argument. If the typical person who introduces anyone introduces bbb people, the number reachable within kkk steps grows like

N(k)≈∑i=1kb i≈bkN(k) \approx \sum_{i=1}^{k} b^{\,i} \approx b^{k}N(k)≈i=1∑k​bi≈bk

In my own sheet, the 15 people who introduced anyone introduced about b≈59/15≈3.9b \approx 59/15 \approx 3.9b≈59/15≈3.9 each. At that rate, 3.97≈14,0003.9^{7} \approx 14{,}0003.97≈14,000: seven introductions covers a whole university community several times over. The name was a bet that everyone on campus was within reach if you could see the chains.

What the graph shows

  • One hub dominates. I'm the direct source of 26 of the 59 connections; everyone else fans out from a handful of "connector" friends. Real networks are like this: a few people do most of the introducing.
  • Teams are clusters. Baseball, lacrosse and engineering contacts sit together in subtrees, because they were introduced through the same people.
  • Depth stays shallow. No one is more than 4 introductions away, which is consistent with the branching estimate.

What an app would have needed

  • Mutual data, not one person's spreadsheet. A working graph needs both ends of each edge to opt in.
  • Many parents per node. People meet through several routes. A tree becomes a general graph, and "distance" becomes shortest path.
  • Privacy controls. This spreadsheet holds 60 people's personal details, so none of it can be shown. The graphic above uses only renumbered nodes and coarse tags.
