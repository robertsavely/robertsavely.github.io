Context

Summer 2025 internship at Tietronix Software, on work that drew interest from NASA and industry. This is employer work: no code, documents or data appear here, and technical details are withheld.

The problem

Companies hold large archives of procedural documents (step-by-step instructions, checklists, tables) as old PDFs. In that form they can't be searched, queried or fed to modern systems. Converting one by hand took 4 hours or more, depending on its length.

What I built

An AI-driven ETL pipeline (extract, transform, load) for procedural documents:

  • Extract. Read legacy PDFs, including their tables, with multimodal models.
  • Transform. Classify and structure the content with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), orchestrated with LangChain and LangGraph.
  • Load. Emit structured output that downstream systems can use directly.

Result

Processing time per document fell from 4+ hours to under 10 minutes. The remaining time is set by compute and model inference, not by the pipeline's logic.

Why it matters

Once legacy procedures are structured data, a company can use them in modern systems: to train employees, to build searchable procedure libraries, and as training and retrieval data for AI models.
