Goal

Our first-semester design brief (team B3): build a sensor station that monitors a plant's environment and acts on it, watering automatically when the soil dries out, while streaming everything to the cloud.

Deployed in the grow lab.(technical report)The electronics before enclosure.(technical report)

System

Wiring: DHT (air temperature and humidity), soil-moisture probe, photocell (light) and a servo driving the water valve.(technical report)

The control logic is a single threshold with hysteresis-free switching. Every reading is published over MQTT with TLS to a HiveMQ Cloud broker under topics like 2023/ENGR111/B/B3/temp:

Control flow: the valve opens when soil humidity drops below 40 %.(technical report) valve={open,hsoil<40%closed,hsoil≥40%\text{valve} = \begin{cases} \text{open}, & h_{\text{soil}} < 40\% \\ \text{closed}, & h_{\text{soil}} \ge 40\% \end{cases}valve={open,closed,​hsoil​<40%hsoil​≥40%​

Publishing is non-blocking (timestamp comparisons instead of delay()), so the sensor loop and network traffic don't stall each other.

Data

Three days of real readings pulled back from the broker:

The soil channel shows the control loop working. Moisture dries down slowly toward the 40 % line; each watering starts with the reading at 40–44 % (the probe reads a few points noisy, so single readings dip to the threshold first), and the reading then jumps back up by 10–15 points (40 → 53 % on December 8, 41 → 56 % on December 9 and 44 → 56 % on December 10). Air humidity and temperature don't jump at those moments, so the steps come from the water, not the room. The air-humidity swing peaking late on December 10 (57 %) is the room itself changing. On December 11 the soil falls below 40 % without a matching step; the log doesn't record why.

SolidWorks assembly drawing of the enclosure and valve mechanism.(technical report)

Improvements

A single 40 % threshold with no hysteresis would make a real valve chatter around the setpoint. A deadband (open below 38 %, close above 42 %) is the obvious next step, and the same idea shows up later in the CPE 345 signal controller's switch margin.
