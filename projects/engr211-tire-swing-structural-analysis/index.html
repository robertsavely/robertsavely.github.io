Problem

ENGR 211's final project was a backyard playground for 5–12-year-olds, analysed for safety under ASTM F1148 loads. My element in our five-person team was a three-rope tire swing hung from a red-maple top beam on an A-frame. The question was how small the members could be while every one still met its target factor of safety under a swinging load.

Group 82's jungle-themed playground (SolidWorks).(final presentation)My element: the A-frame tire swing. Note the triangular top beam.(final presentation)

Loads

The worst case is the bottom of the swing after release from θmax⁡=π/4\theta_{\max} = \pi/4θmax​=π/4. That gives pendulum tension T=mg(3−2cos⁡θ)T = mg(3 - 2\cos\theta)T=mg(3−2cosθ), written in the workbook as

Fdown=W+2 mg (1−cos⁡θmax⁡),W=3 riders×140 lb×2+tire+ropesF_{\text{down}} = W + 2\,m g\,(1-\cos\theta_{\max}), \qquad W = 3\ \text{riders}\times 140\ \text{lb}\times 2 + \text{tire} + \text{ropes}Fdown​=W+2mg(1−cosθmax​),W=3 riders×140 lb×2+tire+ropes

That's 1,372 lb from an 840 lb static rider load. The ropes share it three ways, giving a factor of safety of 4.37 against 2,000 lb-rated HMPE rope (target ≈ 4).

The hand derivation behind the rope check: energy conservation from the release angle to the bottom of the arc.(final presentation)

Sizing as a root-finding problem

I wrote each design condition as a residual that equals zero at the optimum, in a "Formula Box" column, then solve for the zero. For the top beam, a right-isosceles triangular section of side sss (base 2s\sqrt2 s2​s, height s/2s/\sqrt2s/2​):

I=bh336,c=h3,M=PL3+wL28,r(s)=McI−MORFoSI = \frac{b h^3}{36},\quad c = \frac{h}{3},\quad M = \frac{P L}{3} + \frac{w L^2}{8},\qquad r(s) = \frac{M c}{I} - \frac{\text{MOR}}{\text{FoS}}I=36bh3​,c=3h​,M=3PL​+8wL2​,r(s)=IMc​−FoSMOR​

with modulus of rupture 13,400 psi, FoS 3 and span 24 ft.

Shear and moment diagrams for the top beam, the triangular-section inertia I = bh³/36, and the FoS condition, worked by hand before going into the spreadsheet.(final presentation) I solved these graphically in Desmos at the time; those graphs weren't kept. Known issues

  • One shear formula uses COS(45). Excel reads that as 45 radians, not degrees. The rest of the sheet correctly uses PI()/4.
  • The workbook's recorded bending factor (13.8) and the team presentation's (7.57) disagree. Both are preserved as found.
  • Anchor-plate and bolt-shear rows were listed but never computed.

Approach

Writing each requirement as an equation and solving for its zero replaced guess-and-check. Later projects use the same approach.
