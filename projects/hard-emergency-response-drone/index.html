Problem

Responders often arrive at an incident with little visual information: what happened, which approaches are blocked, and what hazards are visible. Project R₀ (team project, IDE 401) proposes an FPV aircraft that gets there first.

OV-1 concept illustration: R₀ takes a direct air path while ground units follow roads, and the scan reaches the responder's tablet before arrival. Concept art, not to scale.(team deck, Oct 2026)

Concept of operations

Dispatch → rapid transit (100 mph target) → scene capture with overlapping views from a single camera → ground-based 3D reconstruction → responders review live imagery and the map → recovery. At the target speed, a 3-mile transit takes about 1.8 minutes, excluding launch, climb and capture.

Why speed matters

A drone only adds value if it arrives with useful imagery before ground units. For a scene ddd miles away, with road-network detour factor κ\kappaκ (road distance ÷ straight-line distance) and launch, climb and capture overhead t0t_0t0​:

Δt=κ dvcar⏟ground−(t0+dvdrone)⏟air\Delta t = \underbrace{\frac{\kappa\, d}{v_{\text{car}}}}_{\text{ground}} - \underbrace{\left(t_0 + \frac{d}{v_{\text{drone}}}\right)}_{\text{air}}Δt=groundvcar​κd​​​−air(t0​+vdrone​d​)​​

At the 100 mph target, the air leg for 3 miles is 1.8 min. The design goal is to make Δt\Delta tΔt large enough that responders can plan their approach from the map. The impacts memo cites Chula Vista's drone program reaching priority-one calls in about 3.5 minutes vs. nearly 8 for patrol cars.

Preliminary architecture

Preliminary airframe concept: nose camera, four motor pods. Concept illustration, not a CAD model.(team deck)Top view.(team deck)

One camera serves both the pilot's FPV feed and the mapping pipeline (HDZero Freestyle V2). The proposed parts are an HDZero Halo flight-controller stack with integrated ELRS receiver and 4-in-1 ESC, T-Motor F90 motors on 7-inch props, a Tattu R-Line battery, and a planned PAHT-CF printed airframe. The ground station handles the video link and reconstruction.

My individual analyses

  • Impacts: response-time effects on clearance rates; drone-delivered AEDs for out-of-hospital cardiac arrest; economic trade-offs.
  • Ethics: equitable deployment across neighbourhoods, limits on surveillance creep, keeping collected data out of commercial use, and measurable indicators (deployment frequency by area, share of flights tied to confirmed calls) to detect misuse.
